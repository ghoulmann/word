# Framework

The framework pages explain the theory behind the ontology — why it is structured as it is, what the five editorial layers mean, and how to use the relationship types to reason about concerns in sequence. The lexicon entries encode the framework's conclusions in structured frontmatter. These pages explain the reasoning.

- **[[five-layers]]** — The five editorial layers (Functional, Computational, Structural, Mechanical, Editorial): what each covers, why the ordering matters, and why a concept may belong to more than one layer.
- **[[triage-sequencing]]** — The triage principle: address concerns top-down through the layers; do not fix commas while structure is unstable. The `prerequisite_for` relationship type and the `address_when` dimension encode this principle.
- **[[category-boundaries]]** — Cascade thresholds: when a Mechanical concern becomes a Structural one, why informal fallacy is Structural rather than Mechanical, and when a single-instance concern escalates to a system-level problem.
- **[[relationship-types]]** — The typed relationship vocabulary: what each relationship type means, how directionality works, and why `increases_risk_of` and `impediment_to` are not synonymous.
