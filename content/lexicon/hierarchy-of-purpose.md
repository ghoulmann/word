# Hierarchy of Purpose

Every document has a primary purpose — the single most important thing it is meant to accomplish. It may also have secondary and tertiary purposes that operate within the primary's constraints. The hierarchy of purpose is the explicit ordering of these goals.

Establishing the hierarchy of purpose is a pre-compositional act. Before the first sentence is drafted, the writer should be able to complete: "This document will \_\_\_ so that \_\_\_." That statement is the primary purpose. Everything else is secondary.

## Why Hierarchy, Not Just Purpose

A document may need to accomplish several things. A safety report must document an incident (primary), reassure stakeholders (secondary), and comply with regulatory format requirements (tertiary). These purposes can conflict: the most comprehensive incident documentation may not be the most reassuring. The hierarchy resolves the conflict: primary purpose governs; secondary purposes operate within its constraints; tertiary purposes operate within the constraints of both primary and secondary.

Without an explicit hierarchy, writers often serve all purposes equally — which means serving none of them well.

## Relationship to Genre and Structure

[[Genre]] is the form-convention contract between writer and reader. The hierarchy of purpose informs genre selection: what the document is *for* determines what *kind* of document it should be.

[[Rhetorical-arrangement]] — the organizational strategy — is also dependent on the hierarchy of purpose. Different purposes call for different arrangements: a document meant to persuade is arranged differently than one meant to instruct, which is arranged differently than one meant to explain.

Both genre and arrangement are `prerequisite_for`-downstream from hierarchy-of-purpose in the triage sequence.

## When Purpose Is Wrong

A document with a misidentified or absent purpose hierarchy produces structural failures that cannot be fixed at lower layers. If the document is designed to inform but the reader needs to be persuaded, the structural arrangement, the selection of content, and the rhetorical choices are all calibrated to the wrong goal. No amount of mechanical or editorial revision will fix a functional purpose failure.

This is the primary example of Layer 1 cascade: functional misalignment produces structural incoherence because the organizing logic is wrong.

## Engineering Design Parallel

In engineering design, hierarchy of purpose corresponds directly to the *define* stage: establishing the problem statement, the design criteria, and the hierarchy of constraints (must-haves vs. nice-to-haves vs. constraints). Engineering design without a clear problem statement is as directionless as writing without a purpose hierarchy. Every downstream decision is made against the wrong target.
