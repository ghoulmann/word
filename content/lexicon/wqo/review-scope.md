# Review Scope

Review scope is the specification of what a review is for. A reviewer asked to "look at this document" has no guidance about whether to comment on structural organization, factual accuracy, prose style, tone, accessibility, or completeness. The result is either a review that focuses on whatever the reviewer notices first, or a review that attempts to cover everything and covers nothing adequately.

Explicit review scope allows reviewers to apply expertise appropriately, calibrate the depth of their review, and skip areas that others are reviewing or that are intentionally deferred.

## Review types and what each requires

**Structural review**: Does the document cover the right topics? Are sections in the right order? Is anything missing or redundant? Reviewer needs the document outline and an understanding of the intended audience and purpose.

**Factual review**: Are the technical claims accurate? Does the code work? Are the procedures correct? Reviewer needs domain expertise and access to the system or sources being documented.

**Mechanical review**: Is the prose grammatically correct, consistent in style, and free of errors? Reviewer needs a copy of the style guide and clear authority to change prose.

**Accessibility review**: Does the document meet accessibility requirements for its publication format? Reviewer needs accessibility criteria and possibly assistive technology access.

## Relationship to feedback quality

[[feedback-actionability]] is the quality of individual feedback items; review scope is the structural condition that makes actionable feedback possible. Without defined scope, even well-intentioned feedback may address the wrong layer.
