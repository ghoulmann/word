# Mechanical Editing

Mechanical editing is the phase of revision that targets correctness at the word and sentence level. It is sequenced last in the editing hierarchy — after structural and substantive concerns are resolved — because sentence-level corrections to a passage that will be reorganized or deleted are wasted effort.

The term encompasses several overlapping practices:

- **Proofreading**: error detection against an established standard (house style, grammar rules)
- **Copyediting**: error correction plus consistency enforcement (terminology, capitalization, hyphenation)
- **Line editing**: sentence-level revision for clarity and concision, operating at the boundary between mechanical and stylistic editing

## Sequencing and dependency

[[coherence]], [[rhetorical-arrangement]], and [[unity]] are structural concerns that must be resolved before mechanical editing begins. A paragraph that is logically coherent but grammatically rough should be edited structurally first; applying mechanical corrections to a paragraph that will be moved or deleted wastes editing effort and may introduce errors during reorganization.

This sequencing is the practical argument for the WQO layer hierarchy: mechanical layer (correctness) is addressed only after structural and functional layers are stable.

## Scope

Mechanical editing addresses the full mechanical layer of WQO: [[grammar-syntax]], [[punctuation]], [[orthography]], [[nominalization]], and [[passive-voice]]. It does not address argument structure, logical validity, or audience calibration — those belong to structural and functional editing passes.
