# Structural Outline

A structural outline is a skeleton of the document's argument and organization, created before drafting. It establishes section headings, their hierarchy, the logical relationship between sections, and the primary claim or function of each section — before any prose is written.

The outline serves as the cheapest possible structural intervention. Moving a section in an outline takes seconds; moving a section in a finished draft requires rewriting transitions, updating cross-references, re-evaluating the flow, and potentially reorganizing subordinate content. Arrangement decisions made at the outline stage cost orders of magnitude less than the same decisions made during revision.

## What a useful outline includes

Beyond a list of headings, a useful outline includes:
- The purpose of each section (what question it answers, what task it enables)
- The logical relationship between adjacent sections (prerequisite, contrast, elaboration, example)
- Any sections that are required by the [[topic-type]] (what a tutorial must include, what a reference entry must document)
- The order argument — why this section comes before that one

## Relationship to rhetorical arrangement

[[rhetorical-arrangement]] is the concern (does the published document present information in the optimal order?); the structural outline is the prewriting practice that addresses it. An outline that correctly sequences sections by logical dependency and reader need is the primary means of achieving rhetorical arrangement — it is much harder to achieve good arrangement through revision than through planning.
