# Inclusivity

Inclusivity in writing means the text does not implicitly exclude or disadvantage readers through its assumptions about who they are. This operates at multiple levels: gendered pronouns and titles, culturally specific metaphors and examples, ability-based idioms ("turn a blind eye," "falling on deaf ears"), and socioeconomic assumptions embedded in what the text treats as obvious or shared.

[[localization]] is the WQO concern that most directly addresses inclusivity at scale: adapting content for specific cultural and linguistic contexts requires making explicit the assumptions embedded in source text. What reads as neutral in one context reads as exclusionary in another.

[[audience-awareness]] is the upstream prerequisite: writers who have accurately modeled their audience — its diversity, its background assumptions, its range of abilities and identities — make better decisions about language choice, examples, and register.

Inclusivity is an editorial-layer concern because it operates through word choice, idiom, and framing rather than through argument structure or logical correctness. It is addressed during drafting and revision, and enforced through style guide provisions and editorial review.
