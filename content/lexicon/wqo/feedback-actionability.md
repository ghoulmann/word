# Feedback Actionability

Actionable feedback is feedback that a writer can directly implement: it identifies the location of a problem, explains what is wrong and why, and suggests a direction for improvement. Non-actionable feedback is correct in its judgment but leaves the writer uncertain about what to change: "This section is confusing" does not tell the writer which sentence, which word, which structural choice, or which assumption produced the confusion.

The distinction matters at scale. A document with 20 non-actionable comments requires the author to interpret each comment before acting on it — effectively doing the reviewer's analytical work after the review. A document with 20 actionable comments can be revised more directly, with the author applying judgment only where the reviewer's suggestion doesn't fit.

## Characteristics of actionable feedback

**Located**: Identifies a specific sentence, phrase, section, or heading — not "the second half" or "throughout."

**Diagnostic**: Explains what the problem is, not just that a problem exists. "This sentence is passive" is more actionable than "the prose feels distant."

**Directional**: Suggests a direction for improvement, even if not a complete solution. "Could this be rewritten with the agent as subject?" is actionable; "unclear" is not.

**Scoped**: Distinguishes between a pattern that recurs throughout and an isolated instance.

## Relationship to review scope

[[review-scope]] defines what will be reviewed; feedback actionability determines the quality of what is produced. Both are necessary: clear scope without actionable feedback produces a focused review that authors cannot implement; actionable feedback without clear scope may address the wrong layer.
