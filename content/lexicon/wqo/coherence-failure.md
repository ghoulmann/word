# Coherence Failure

Coherence failure occurs when a text's logical thread breaks — when the reader cannot determine how a sentence or section follows from what preceded it. Unlike a local grammatical error, coherence failure is global: it disrupts the reader's working model of the argument, requiring backtracking or forcing inference that may be incorrect.

[[coherence]] as a WQO concern describes the property; coherence failure is its absence. Several mechanical and structural concerns increase the risk of coherence failure:

- **[[nominalization]]** obscures agency and causal relationships, making it harder to track which entity caused or performed what action across sentences.
- **[[passive-voice]]** suppresses agents systematically, producing prose in which subjects and causes are ambiguous or absent.
- **[[informal-fallacy|Informal fallacies]]** are a structural subtype of coherence failure: when an argument's logical connective is invalid, the chain of reasoning breaks regardless of how fluently the prose reads.

Coherence failure is distinct from local confusion. A reader can encounter an unfamiliar term without losing coherence; coherence failure means the logical structure itself has collapsed, and no amount of vocabulary knowledge will restore it.
