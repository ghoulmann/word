# Sentence Efficiency

Sentence efficiency measures how much meaning a sentence delivers per word. An efficient sentence contains no words that could be removed without loss of meaning. An inefficient sentence contains words that repeat, hedge without purpose, or restate what context already supplies.

[[lard-factor]] is the operationalization of sentence efficiency as a measurable property: the ratio of deadwood words to total words. High lard factor means low sentence efficiency.

The primary threats to sentence efficiency are [[nominalization]] (which inflates word count by converting single verbs into multi-word noun phrases) and [[weak-words]] (which add volume without adding meaning). Restoring verbs and removing vague intensifiers ("very," "quite," "rather," "somewhat") are the two highest-yield efficiency moves in revision.

[[semantic-density]] is the related concept at the paragraph or document level: the ratio of distinctive informational content to total words, relevant especially in contexts where token count matters (AI processing, indexed retrieval).
