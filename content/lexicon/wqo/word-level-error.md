# Word-Level Error

A word-level error is a localized failure at the lexical or morphological level: a misspelled word, an incorrect inflection (*"we was"*), a wrong but plausible word form (*"affect"* vs. *"effect"*), or a malapropism. Word-level errors are distinct from syntactic errors, which involve clause or phrase structure, and from semantic errors, which involve inappropriate word choice for the intended meaning.

[[orthography]] is the WQO concern governing spelling and capitalization conventions — the most common source of word-level errors in edited prose.

[[terminology-consistency]] becomes relevant when word-level errors manifest as inconsistent naming: using *"API key"* in one section and *"access token"* in another when both refer to the same credential. This is not an orthographic error but a precision error with similar surface characteristics.

The [[paramedic-method]] does not address word-level errors directly — it operates at the clause level — so word-level correction belongs to the mechanical editing pass, after structural revision is complete.
