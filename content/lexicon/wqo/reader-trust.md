# Reader Trust

Reader trust is not granted — it is built incrementally through every encounter with a document and lost rapidly when a reader detects an error, inconsistency, or logical failure. Trust operates as a precondition: a reader who does not trust a text will not act on it, recommend it, or return to it, regardless of how accurate its central claims may be.

## How trust is built and lost

Trust accumulates from signals distributed throughout a text. Consistent terminology tells the reader the author controlled their language deliberately. Current information tells the reader the author maintained the document. Valid links tell the reader the author verified their references. Clear argument structure tells the reader the author reasoned carefully.

Each WQO concern that erodes trust does so through a specific signal:

- **[[asset-validity]]** failures (broken links, missing images) signal neglect — if the author did not notice a broken reference, what else did they miss?
- **[[informal-fallacy|Informal fallacies]]** signal flawed reasoning — a reader who catches one begins scrutinizing every claim.
- **[[temporal-accuracy]]** failures (stale dates, superseded procedures) signal that the document is not maintained and may no longer reflect reality.
- **[[weak-words]]** (hedging, vague intensifiers) signal low confidence in the claims being made, which the reader interprets as uncertainty about their truth.
- **[[cognitive-overload]]** is trust-corrosive indirectly: a text that exhausts the reader signals that the author did not edit for the reader's benefit.

## Trust and consequential documents

In high-stakes contexts — medical documentation, legal agreements, safety procedures, financial disclosures — reader trust is not merely a quality dimension; it is a compliance and liability issue. Readers of consequential documents perform implicit credibility audits before acting. A document that fails those audits will not be followed.
