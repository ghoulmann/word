# Audience Profiling

Audience profiling is the prewriting activity of systematically characterizing the intended reader before composing. Where [[audience-awareness]] is the concern (does the document demonstrate knowledge of its reader?), audience profiling is the process that produces that knowledge.

Profiling answers: Who will read this? What do they already know? What do they need to do? What context will they bring? What will prevent them from understanding? Without this analysis, audience calibration is guesswork — the author imagines a reader who resembles themselves rather than the actual reader.

## Profiling dimensions

**Knowledge level**: Domain expert, practitioner, or novice? What terminology can be assumed? What analogies will connect to their existing knowledge?

**Goal and task**: What will the reader do with this content? Readers who are following a procedure have different needs from readers building a conceptual model.

**Context of use**: Will they read linearly or scan? Will they arrive via search or follow a link from a support ticket? Will they be time-pressured?

**Audience sensitivity**: Does the audience include readers with cognitive disabilities, second-language learners, screen reader users, or cross-cultural readers? These affect vocabulary, sentence length, structural complexity, and visual design.

## Relationship to rhetoric

[[rhetorical-situation]] determines the exigence, constraints, and context; audience profiling is the component of situational analysis focused specifically on the receiver. A complete rhetorical situation analysis includes audience profiling as one of its inputs.
