# By Editorial Layer

The five editorial layers are one classification axis — Dimension 1 (D1) — not the organizing principle of the ontology. Each layer names a *kind* of editorial concern. A concept may belong to more than one layer. See [[five-layers]] for the theory.

---

## [[functional|Functional]]
*Purpose and audience: does this text do what it needs to do for its reader?*

Pre-compositional and document-level concerns. Must be resolved before lower-layer editing is meaningful.

- [[audience-awareness]] — identifying and calibrating to the reader
- [[hierarchy-of-purpose]] — the ordered goals the document must accomplish
- [[genre]] — the form-convention contract with the reader
- [[topic-type]] — Diataxis classification: tutorial, how-to, reference, explanation
- [[asset-validity]] — integrity of external resources (also Computational)
- [[controlled-vocabulary]] — authoritative term set for a documentation system (also Computational)

---

## [[computational|Computational]]
*Pipeline performance: does this text process correctly in automated systems?*

Concerns that govern how text behaves in digital, retrieval, and AI processing contexts.

- [[tokenomics]] — token efficiency in language model contexts
- [[chunking]] — structuring content for retrieval and segmentation
- [[asset-validity]] — integrity of links, images, and embedded resources (also Functional)
- [[controlled-vocabulary]] — term standardization for search and retrieval (also Functional)

---

## [[structural|Structural]]
*Logic and architecture: does the argument hold together?*

Paragraph and document-scale concerns. Evaluated by human judgment on logical relationships.

- [[coherence]] — logical continuity across claims
- [[cohesion]] — surface-level textual connection
- [[unity]] — every element of a unit serving one purpose
- [[boundary-control]] — discipline over what belongs in each unit
- [[rhetorical-arrangement]] — organizational strategy matching content to purpose
- [[rhetorical-mode]] — the structural pattern governing a passage
- [[informal-fallacy]] — logical errors in argument structure
- [[hierarchy-of-purpose]] — ordered goals governing all choices (also Functional)
- [[ethos-pathos-logos]] — modes of rhetorical appeal

---

## [[mechanical|Mechanical]]
*Correctness: does the text conform to the universal rules of the language?*

Rule-governed and risk-factor concerns at sentence and word level. The domain of copy editing and automated linting.

- [[grammar-syntax]] — correctness of grammatical structures
- [[punctuation]] — rule-governed sentence marking
- [[orthography]] — spelling and capitalization
- [[nominalization]] — verb-to-noun conversion (also Structural at cascade)
- [[passive-voice]] — agent-absent construction
- [[lard-factor]] — deadwood ratio; compression measure
- [[weak-words]] — low-content vocabulary
- [[readability-metrics]] — computational reading difficulty proxies
- [[cognitive-overload]] — working memory demands (mediating impact; also Structural)

---

## [[editorial|Editorial]]
*Appropriateness: does the text conform to the applicable conventions for its context?*

Convention-dependent and context-dependent concerns. Governed by house style and audience calibration.

- [[house-style]] — codified organizational conventions
- [[tone-voice-cadence]] — affective register and rhythmic quality
- [[information-design]] — visual and spatial organization of content
- [[localization]] — adaptation for cultural and linguistic context
- [[terminology-consistency]] — uniform use of approved terms
- [[genre]] — convention governance (also Functional)
- [[e-prime]] — constraint writing philosophy (also convention-level)
- [[plain-language]] — multi-layer philosophy (spans Structural, Mechanical, Editorial)
