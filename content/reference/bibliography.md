# Bibliography

Sources that informed the concepts, classification, and relationships in this ontology. Organized by domain.

---

## Rhetoric and Composition

**Aristotle.** *Rhetoric*. Translated by W. Rhys Roberts. — Foundation text for ethos/pathos/logos and rhetorical arrangement. The three modes of persuasion and the five canons of rhetoric (invention, arrangement, style, memory, delivery) remain the organizing framework of rhetorical analysis.

**Lanham, Richard A.** *Revising Prose*. 5th ed. Pearson Longman, 2006. — Source of the paramedic method, lard factor, and the systematic critique of nominalization and bureaucratic prose. The paramedic method is the primary operationalization of plain language at the sentence level in this ontology.

**Lanham, Richard A.** *Style: An Anti-Textbook*. Paul Dry Books, 2007. — Companion argument for the value of ornate style alongside functional clarity; establishes that "plain" and "clear" are context-dependent, not universal standards.

**Orwell, George.** "Politics and the English Language." 1946. — Classic argument for plain, concrete, specific prose over bureaucratic abstraction. Orwell's six rules anticipate many of the mechanical concerns in this ontology.

## Technical Writing and Documentation

**Procida, Daniele.** *Diátaxis: A Systematic Approach to Technical Documentation Architecture*. diataxis.fr. — Source of the topic-type classification (tutorial, how-to, reference, explanation). The Diataxis framework is the primary model for boundary control at document scale in this ontology.

**Redish, Janice "Ginny".** *Letting Go of the Words: Writing Web Content That Works*. 2nd ed. Morgan Kaufmann, 2012. — Foundational text for web-focused plain language: audience analysis, chunking, information architecture, and reading-level calibration for digital contexts.

**Hackos, JoAnn T., and Dawn M. Stevens.** *Standards for Online Communication*. Wiley, 1997. — Technical documentation standards; classification of content types predating Diataxis.

---

## Plain Language and Accessibility

**Federal Plain Language Guidelines.** PlainLanguage.gov. Plain Language Action and Information Network (PLAIN). — The US government's codified plain language requirements under the Plain Writing Act of 2010. Primary reference for operationalizing plain language in government and technical writing.

**US Department of Health and Human Services.** *Research-Based Web Design and Usability Guidelines*. — Evidence base for information design and web usability conventions.

**Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1.** W3C Recommendation. — Technical standard for accessibility-technical and accessibility-presentation concerns. Minimum contrast ratios, semantic HTML requirements, and alt text specifications.

---

## Language, Grammar, and Style

**Garner, Bryan A.** *Garner's Modern English Usage*. 4th ed. Oxford University Press, 2016. — Authoritative reference for contested usage, grammar, and style; grounded in corpus linguistics.

**Chicago Manual of Style*. 17th ed. University of Chicago Press, 2017. — Primary house-style reference for academic and general publishing.

**AP Stylebook.** Associated Press, updated annually. — Primary house-style reference for journalism.

**Korzybski, Alfred.** *Science and Sanity*. 4th ed. International Non-Aristotelian Library Publishing, 1958. — Source of the general semantics framework that Bourland extended into E-Prime; "the map is not the territory."

**Bourland, D. David, Jr.** "A Linguistic Note: Writing in E-Prime." *General Semantics Bulletin*, 1965. — Original proposal for E-Prime as a variant of English eliminating "to be."

## Engineering Design

**Engineering Design Process.** — The iterative design process taught across engineering programs. Stages: define, research, brainstorm, choose, build, test, improve, communicate. Used here as an organizational lens for the engineering-design navigational view.

**Evans, Eric.** *Domain-Driven Design: Tackling Complexity in the Heart of Software.* Addison-Wesley, 2003. — Source of the "ubiquitous language" concept — the controlled vocabulary parallel for software engineering teams.

---

## Linguistics and Discourse Analysis

**Halliday, M.A.K., and Ruqaiya Hasan.** *Cohesion in English*. Longman, 1976. — Foundational work defining the linguistic devices of cohesion: reference, substitution, ellipsis, conjunction, lexical cohesion.

**van Dijk, Teun A.** *Text and Context: Explorations in the Semantics and Pragmatics of Discourse*. Longman, 1977. — Framework for coherence analysis at discourse level.

---

## Natural Language Processing and AI Writing

**tiktoken.** OpenAI. github.com/openai/tiktoken — Reference tokenizer for understanding how text is tokenized by OpenAI models; primary tool for tokenomics analysis.

**NLTK WordNet.** Natural Language Toolkit. — Source of pre-pulled definition, synonym, and related form data embedded in lexicon frontmatter via `scripts/pull_wordnet.py`.
